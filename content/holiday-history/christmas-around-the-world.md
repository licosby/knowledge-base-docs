# Christmas Around the World

## Overview
Christmas looks very different depending on where you are in the world. Some places celebrate with winter markets and warm drinks. Others celebrate with fireworks, beach trips, or even fast food traditions. Every country has its own mix of history, food, and folklore that shapes the holiday season. This page gives a simple look at how different cultures celebrate Christmas and how these traditions have grown over time.

# 🇩🇪 Germany and 🇦🇹 Austria

Germany and Austria celebrate Christmas with some of the oldest and most famous winter traditions in Europe. Their Christmas markets run from late November through December and fill city squares with lights, warm drinks, and holiday foods. Alongside these cozy celebrations, the region is also known for its Alpine folklore, including the famous Krampus who appears in early December. Together, these traditions lead into Advent and continue through Christmas Eve and Christmas Day, creating a season filled with family gatherings, music, and winter festivities.

### Christmas Markets
Christmas markets in Germany and Austria date back to the Middle Ages. The Dresden Striezelmarkt began in 1434 and is one of the oldest recorded markets. These markets are known for warm drinks, gingerbread, roasted nuts, handmade toys, and bright lights that fill the streets. Cities such as Vienna, Munich, Nuremberg, and Salzburg host large markets that attract visitors from around the world.[^germany-markets]

### Krampus and Alpine Folklore

Krampus is one of the most dramatic and unforgettable parts of the Alpine Christmas season. While Germany and Austria are known for cozy markets, warm drinks, and soft lights, the mountains add a wilder twist with Krampus, a horned creature from old folklore who appears in early December. He is traditionally connected to Saint Nicholas and was said to scare misbehaving children while Saint Nicholas rewarded the good ones. Today, Krampus is more festive than frightening and has become a major part of winter celebrations.

Krampus parades, called Krampuslauf, take place in early December and are loud, energetic, and full of excitement. People dress in carved wooden masks, fur covered costumes, jingling bells, and clattering chains as they run or march through the streets. Crowds gather to watch the chaos, cheer, and enjoy the mix of folklore and fun. These parades have grown in popularity and now appear in other countries as well, but the most famous celebrations still take place in the Alpine regions of Austria and Bavaria.[^krampus]

# 🇳🇱 The Netherlands

The Netherlands have two major Christmas celebrations. One is centered on Sinterklaas and the other is the quieter Christmas holiday that takes place later in December. Dutch Christmas markets run from late November through December, and people come from all over to enjoy the holiday season with Dutch treats, lights, and warm gluhwein. These markets lead into the Sinterklaas festivities on December 5 and then continue into the second Christmas celebration that begins on December 24.


### Christmas 1: Sinterklaasavond  
Sinterklaas is said to live in Spain and arrives in the Netherlands by boat in mid November. On the night of December 4 children place shoes by the fireplace and leave carrots for his horse. Sinterklaas then leaves small gifts or sweets. Families celebrate on December 5 with poems, chocolate letters, and pepernoten which are small spiced cookies. This is the main gift giving holiday in the Netherlands and is full of songs, treats, and family traditions.[^sinterklaas]

---

### Christmas 2: Kerst  
The second Christmas celebration takes place on December 24 through December 26.  
-Kerstavond is Christmas Eve.  
-Eerste Kerstdag is First Christmas Day.  
-Tweede Kerstdag is Second Christmas Day.

This celebration is calm and cozy. Families focus on spending time together, enjoying long meals, lighting candles, and relaxing at home. Second Christmas Day is often used for visiting extended family, going out to eat, shopping, or simply enjoying a quiet day together. Gifts are not the main focus because Sinterklaas already covers that earlier in the month.[^kerst]

### Stroopwafels at Markets
During the Christmas season, Dutch winter markets fill with warm drinks, lights, and sweet treats, and stroopwafels are one of the most popular things people enjoy there. Stroopwafels are a Dutch treat made from two thin waffle cookies with warm caramel syrup in the center. They originated in Gouda in the eighteenth century and became a popular winter market food. People often warm them over a cup of coffee so the caramel softens. Stroopwafels are now sold worldwide but remain a favorite at Dutch holiday markets. The traditional caramel center is still the favorite, but stroopwafels now come in many flavors and fillings. [^stroopwafel]

#### Popular Stroopwafel Flavors

* Classic Caramel  
  The original favorite with warm gooey caramel that softens perfectly over a hot drink.
* Chocolate  
  A rich chocolate filling that makes the stroopwafel taste like a cookie and candy combined.
* Honey  
  A lighter and floral sweetness that feels smooth and natural.
* Speculaas Spice  
  A Dutch holiday flavor with cinnamon, nutmeg, and clove that tastes like Christmas.
* Coffee Caramel  
  A deeper caramel with a hint of coffee that pairs perfectly with a warm mug.
* Maple  
  Sweet and buttery with a flavor similar to breakfast syrup.
* Vanilla  
  Soft and mellow with a gentle sweetness that keeps things simple.
* Hazelnut  
  Nutty and smooth with a flavor similar to chocolate hazelnut spread.
* Salted Caramel  
  A sweet and salty twist that has become a modern favorite.
* Chocolate Dipped  
  A classic stroopwafel dipped in chocolate for extra crunch and sweetness.

# 🇮🇸 Iceland

Iceland celebrates Christmas with a mix of folklore, winter traditions, and family gatherings. The holiday season begins in early December and continues through Christmas Eve and Christmas Day. Towns decorate with lights to brighten the long winter nights, and families enjoy warm meals, storytelling, and visits from the Yule Lads, who appear throughout the season with playful mischief.

### Icelandic Winter Folklore
Iceland has winter folklore that shares some similarities with Alpine traditions. Gryla is a giant who listens for misbehaving children and the mother of the Yule Lads. The Christmas Cat is said to appear if people do not receive new clothing for the holiday. These stories were used to encourage good behavior during long winter months and are now shared in a lighter and more playful way.[^iceland-folklore]

### Yule Lads
The Yule Lads are thirteen figures from Icelandic folklore who visit children during the Christmas season. Each one has a playful or mischievous personality. Children place shoes out for small gifts. The Yule Lads are part of a larger folklore family that includes their mother Gryla and the Christmas Cat.[^yulelads]

# 🇲🇽 Latin America
Across Latin America, Christmas is celebrated with lively traditions, bright decorations, and large family gatherings. The season begins in early December and leads into Nochebuena on December 24, which is the main night of celebration. Communities enjoy fireworks, music, and holiday foods, and families gather late into the night for meals, church services, and gift giving.

### Nochebuena
Nochebuena takes place on December 24 and focuses on family gatherings and shared meals. Many families attend a late evening church service called Misa de Gallo. Afterward they celebrate with music, lights, food, and children open presents at midnight.[^nochebuena]

### Food Traditions
Families prepare dishes such as arepas, hallacas, and sweet treats like flan or tres leches cake. These foods reflect a blend of Indigenous, Spanish, and local influences that have shaped holiday meals over centuries.[^latin-food]

# 🇯🇵 Asia

Many countries across Asia celebrate Christmas in their own unique ways, blending local customs with global holiday traditions. Christmas markets, light festivals, and community celebrations begin in early December, especially in places like Japan, South Korea, Singapore, and the Philippines. People enjoy seasonal foods, bright decorations, and public events that bring cities to life. These celebrations lead into Christmas Eve and Christmas Day, which are often focused on family gatherings, festive meals, and cultural traditions that reflect each region’s style of celebrating.

### Japan’s KFC Christmas
Japan has a unique Christmas tradition that began in the nineteen seventies when a marketing campaign encouraged families to enjoy Kentucky Fried Chicken for Christmas dinner. Many people place orders weeks in advance. Christmas in Japan focuses on lights, shared meals, and gift giving rather than religious celebrations.[^kfc-japan]

#### Popular Christmas Foods in Japan
- KFC holiday meals  
- Christmas cake  
- Seasonal desserts

# 🇦🇺 Australia

### Summer Christmas
Christmas in Australia takes place during summer. Families celebrate with beach trips, barbecues, and outdoor gatherings. Australia’s Christmas traditions blend European holiday customs with the reality of summer weather. [^australia-summer] 

Instead of heavy winter meals, families choose:
* chilled dishes
* fresh produce
* cold desserts
* seafood that is easy to prepare

### Surfing Santa
Surfing Santa is a playful part of Australian Christmas culture. Some coastal towns host events where people dress as Santa and surf for charity or fun. The image appears in decorations and advertisements and reflects the warm weather and beach lifestyle of the region.[^surfing-santa]

![Surfing Santa at Soldiers Beach, Australia](assets/images/surfing-santa.jpeg)

### Boxing Day
Boxing Day is celebrated on December 26 and is a big part of the Christmas season in Australia. The day originally began as a time to give boxed gifts or donations to workers and people in need, but it has grown into a relaxed holiday that feels like an easygoing extension of Christmas.  Many people also take part in Boxing Day traditions like shopping sales or watching major sporting events. It is a calm and sunny holiday that keeps the festive spirit going without any of the rush.
