# History of Christmas Decorations

## Overview
Christmas decorations have changed a lot over the years, and each tradition has its own story. People started with simple greenery and candles, and eventually moved into colorful lights, ornaments, and outdoor displays. Some decorations come from old winter customs, and others were created much later as technology and creativity grew. This page looks at how Christmas decorating began, how it became a yearly tradition, and why certain decorations are still used today. It is a mix of history, culture, and the fun parts of celebrating the season.

## Outdoor Christmas Lights
### When We Started Decorating Houses
Outdoor Christmas house decorating didn’t really begin until the early 20th century. People had been putting candles on trees for generations, but lighting the outside of a home wasn’t practical until electric bulbs became safer and weather‑resistant. Early electric Christmas lights appeared in the 1880s thanks to Edison’s company, but they were mostly used indoors or on public trees. The idea of decorating houses slowly grew as electricity spread into neighborhoods, and by the late 1920s and 1930s, families began adding simple strands around porches and windows. The real boom came after World War II, when suburban homes, mass‑produced lights, and a growing sense of community turned outdoor decorating into a beloved tradition. By the 1950s, entire streets were glowing with color, and friendly neighborhood “who has the best lights” competitions became part of the holiday season. Today, decorating houses is one of the most joyful and recognizable Christmas customs.


### Global Outdoor Light Traditions
Around the world, cities and cultural landmarks create spectacular Christmas light displays that attract millions of visitors each year. Many of these traditions began as small community celebrations and grew into major seasonal events supported by tourism, local businesses, and city governments. Some displays, like Tivoli Gardens in Denmark, date back more than a century, while others are modern artistic installations. These illuminated attractions boost local economies, inspire holiday travel, and showcase each region’s unique approach to celebrating the season. Below are some of the most famous global light traditions.

- Denmark: Tivoli Gardens  
- Italy: Luci d’Artista (Turin & Salerno)  
- Austria: Vienna Christmas Lights  
- Germany: Berlin Christmas Garden  
- Netherlands: Amsterdam Light Festival  
- United States: Dyker Heights Christmas Lights (Brooklyn, NY)  
- Mexico: Mexico City Zócalo Christmas Lights

## Christmas Lights as Big Business
### Professional Light Hanging Services
Christmas lights have become such a big part of the season that entire companies exist just to hang them. It is honestly kind of amazing. Some people love decorating but do not have the time, patience, or ladder climbing confidence to do it themselves. Others are older or simply want their house to look festive without wrestling with tangled cords or icy rooftops. Professional installers handle everything from planning the design to putting the lights up and taking them down when the season ends. It is convenient, it is safer, and it keeps the fun parts of decorating while skipping the stressful ones. Over the years this has grown into a huge seasonal industry, and you can find services for simple roofline lights or full synchronized displays that look like they belong on television. 

### The Great Christmas Light Fight
The Great Christmas Light Fight aired on ABC from 2013 through 2025 and quickly became a holiday favorite. Each episode featured families competing to create the most spectacular Christmas display with a prize of fifty thousand dollars. Many contestants spent far more than that on decorations because they loved the tradition and the joy it brought to their neighborhoods. The show highlighted creativity, engineering, and the dedication behind large scale displays. It turned holiday decorating into a national event and inspired many people to expand their own setups.

### Pop Culture References
Holiday movies love showing the chaos and comedy of decorating homes.
-Christmas with the Kranks features a neighborhood that demands the Kranks put out their traditional giant Frosty the Snowman.
-National Lampoon’s Christmas Vacation follows Clark Griswold as he battles frozen gutters, stubborn staples, and a mountain of tangled lights in his quest to impress his family.
-Deck the Halls turns decorating into a full rivalry between Matthew Broderick and Danny DeVito, with the dream of making a house visible from space.

These movies capture the fun, the frustration, and the spectacle of Christmas lights, which is exactly why people love them.
