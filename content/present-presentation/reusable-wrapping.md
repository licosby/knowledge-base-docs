# Reusable Wrapping  
<h4><b>How to wrap gifts without creating a giant trash avalanche</b></h4>
Most people don’t realize how much waste Christmas wrapping creates until they’re staring at a living room that looks like a paper factory exploded. Switching to reusable wrapping is basically the cheat code for making Christmas prettier, cheaper, and way less wasteful.

---

Every year, we toss out **huge** amounts of wrapping paper, tape, ribbons, and packaging. A lot of it *can’t* be recycled because it’s shiny, glittery, laminated, or covered in plastic.[^1][^2][^3]

Some highlights from recent waste studies:
- Millions of tons of wrapping paper get thrown away every Christmas season.[^4][^5]
- Glitter, foil, and laminated paper are not recyclable.[^1][^2]
- Plastic ribbons and bows usually end up in landfills.[^3]
- The amount of tape used at Christmas could wrap around the Earth hundreds of times.[^6]

Traditional wrapping is cute for 10 minutes and then becomes trash for 100 years. Reusable wrapping fixes that.

## Don't Use This, Use This Instead

- **Anything covered in glitter**  
  Most glitter used for packaging and decoration is made out of polyester, it multiplies in your house and you will find it all year long in the most strange places, it never breaks down, and never looks good after the first use. It instantly turns your wrapping into single‑use trash.

- **Foil, laminated, or metallic papers**  
  They’re pretty, but they can’t be recycled and they crease permanently. If you want something shiny, use a laminated photo or keepsake tag instead- something meant to last, not wrap.

- **Plastic bows**  
  They fall off, get crushed, and can’t be reused in any meaningful way. Skip them and use ribbon or fabric bows that can survive more than one holiday.

- **Tape‑heavy wrapping styles**  
  Tape makes paper tear when opened, which means it can’t be reused. Use neat folds and tucked edges instead.

- **Anything that tears easily**  
  Thin, cheap paper won’t survive a second wrapping. If it rips the moment someone touches it, it’s not reusable.

# Types of Reusable Wrapping

#### Fabric Wraps
Think: furoshiki cloths, scarves, bandanas, tea towels, table runners.  
They look fancy, tie beautifully, and last forever.
#### Reusable Gift Bags
The easiest option.  
You literally just drop the gift in, tie the handles, and boom — done.
#### Tins, Boxes & Jars
Metal tins, wooden boxes, mason jars, cookie tins…  
These make the gift feel extra special and can be reused for storage.
#### Cloth Pouches
Drawstring bags made from cotton, linen, velvet, or felt.  
They feel very “storybook Christmas."
# Wrapping Gifts *With* a Gift

- Wrap a book in a blanket.  
- Wrap jewelry in a scarf.  
- Wrap a cookbook in an apron and include kitchen utensils in the pockets. Go one step further and have the apron customized by having it embroidered with their name.  
- Wrap snacks inside a tote bag or in a basket that they can refill when all the goodies have been eaten.  
- Wrap self‑care items inside a cosmetic pouch.  

## Cute Ways to Decorate a Package
- Add a customized keychain (initials, name, or a charm that fits their vibe)
- Tie on a Christmas ornament so it becomes part of their tree later. If it is someone you gift annually you could make it a recurring theme. This is a fun idea for a child to have when they go to decorate their first tree as an adult. Christmas is all about family, 
- Clip a badge reel to the ribbon for a practical little extra
- Tuck a gift card under the bow or into a tiny envelope on the front
- Add a small trinket like an enamel pin, charm, or mini accessory
see also [[package-decorations| package decorations]]

Reusable wrapping doesn’t just save waste, it makes your gifts look like they came straight out of a cozy Christmas movie.

## Why People Love Reusable Wrapping

- It’s cheaper long-term  
- It looks way more aesthetic  
- It’s easier to store  
- It’s fun to collect fabrics and bags  
- It makes your gifts feel intentional  
- You’re not drowning in trash on Christmas morning
