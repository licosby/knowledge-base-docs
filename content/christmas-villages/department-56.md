# Department 56 Christmas Village

![My Dickens Village](village.jpg)
<sub>© 2024 Lindsey Cosby. All rights reserved.</sub>

Department 56 Christmas Villages are collectible miniature holiday displays made up of hand‑painted ceramic buildings, accessories, and figures. Since their introduction in 1976, they have grown into multiple themed village series that reflect different eras, cultures, and Christmas traditions. Collectors use these pieces to build detailed winter scenes, celebrate holiday nostalgia, and preserve the craftsmanship and storytelling that define the brand.

---
# History & Craftsmanship
### Origins

Department 56 is one of the most well known Christmas village brands. The Dickens Village line recreates Victorian England with detailed porcelain buildings inspired by Charles Dickens stories.

##### <u>What Makes Dept 56 Special</u>
• High detail porcelain buildings  
• Historical themes  
• Classic Victorian architecture  
• Collectible limited edition pieces  
• Strong storytelling elements  

#### Early Development

Department 56 began in 1976 as a small gift import division inside Bachman’s, a Minneapolis floral retailer. The idea came from Ed Bazinet, a floral designer who expanded Bachman’s giftware department (internally labeled Department 56).

Bazinet proposed creating small, illuminated ceramic houses after seeing historic Christmas decorations in Stillwater, Minnesota. This led to the release of six hand‑painted buildings that became The Original Snow Village.

The line took off quickly because it offered detailed ceramic buildings, interior lighting, and a nostalgic holiday theme that wasn’t common in mass‑market décor at the time.

# Major Village Series
## The Original Snow Village

The Original Snow Village debuted in 1976 with a small collection of hand‑painted ceramic houses inspired by classic American holiday traditions. Designed to capture the warmth of small‑town Christmas celebrations, the series features illuminated buildings, detailed accessories, and seasonal scenes that have become a long‑standing favorite among collectors.

#### Theme & Style

The Original Snow Village leans into that classic small town American Christmas look of a Hallmark movie. The style is simple and familiar: mid‑century houses, neighborhood shops, and community buildings with warm lighting and small seasonal details. It isn’t fancy or Victorian; it’s more “real life Christmas,” which is exactly why people connect with it.

#### Key Buildings 

These were the original pieces released in 1976, and they set the tone for the whole line:
- **The Mountain Lodge**  
- **The Gabled Cottage**  
- **The Steepled Church**  
- **The Country School**  
- **The Town Hall**  
- **The Victorian House**  

## Dickens’ Village Series

![My Dickens Village](dickens.jpg)
<sub>© 2024 Lindsey Cosby. All rights reserved.</sub>

Dickens’ Village focuses on Victorian England and pulls inspiration from the world of Charles Dickens. The buildings have a more detailed, old world look compared to the American style of Snow Village. Think cobblestone streets, gas lamps, and classic architecture that feels straight out of _A Christmas Carol_. It’s basically the cozy Victorian Christmas version of Department 56.

#### Theme & Style

Dickens’ Village leans hard into Victorian England — the kind you picture when someone says “classic Christmas.” The style is all cobblestone streets, gas lamps, old brick shops, and that slightly dramatic Victorian architecture that looks straight out of a Charles Dickens story. It’s more detailed and old‑world compared to Snow Village, with a cozy, storybook vibe that makes the whole display feel like it belongs in a Christmas novel.

#### Key Buildings

• Scrooge and Marley’s Counting House  
• The Old Curiosity Shop  
• Cratchit’s Corner  
• Victorian shops and homes  
• Churches and town squares

## Licensed & Modern Collections

Department 56 started rolling out licensed pieces in the late 1980s and early 1990s, and it turned out to be a smart move. As collectors wanted more than traditional village buildings, the company began adding scenes from popular movies and TV shows. These special editions brought in a whole new group of customers.

### Movie & TV Tie‑Ins

- National Lampoon’s Christmas Vacation  
- A Christmas Story  
- The Grinch Who Stole Christmas  
- Rudolph the Red‑Nosed Reindeer  
- The Nightmare Before Christmas  
- Harry Potter (select village pieces)  
- Elf (limited releases)  

### Special Editions

- Anniversary Houses 
- Holiday Gift Sets  
- Limited‑Run Lighted Houses  
- Artist‑Signature Pieces  
- Seasonal Figurine Packs  
- Collector’s Exclusive Buildings
